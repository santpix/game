The Atelier

Millinery is patience made visible — felt and straw coaxed over wooden blocks, steamed, shaped and stitched by hand until a silhouette finds its posture.

Marie Galvin has spent two decades making hats and headpieces for weddings, the races, ceremony and everyday wear. Each piece is made in small numbers in New Hampshire, with materials chosen for how they hold light and hold their shape.

Irish Heritage

Irish heritage,
modern millinery.

The work is rooted in an Irish sense of occasion — the hat as something worn with intention, for a wedding, a christening, a race day, a long lunch that turns into an evening. That inheritance is kept, then stripped back.

What remains is line. Brims are cut to frame the face rather than hide it; trims are edited until only one gesture is left. The result reads as contemporary, yet asks to be kept for decades.
